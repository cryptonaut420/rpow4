# Origins / History

A public reading room for the ideas that led to Bitcoin and to this RPOW4
tribute: electronic cash, proof-of-work, cypherpunk politics, smart contracts,
and the paper that tied the pieces together.

Bitcoin did not appear out of nowhere. It fused decades of experiments:
public-key cryptography, digital signatures, blind signatures, timestamping,
proof-of-work, cypherpunk remailers, electronic cash, smart contracts, and
private monetary experiments.

RPOW4 sits in that line as a modern tribute to Hal Finney's RPOW, and to the
later RPOW2 tribute project this codebase descends from.

## Compressed Timeline

1976  Diffie-Hellman public-key exchange opens the door to public cryptographic identity.
1982  David Chaum describes blind signatures for privacy-preserving electronic cash.
1988  Timothy C. May circulates The Crypto Anarchist Manifesto.
1991  Haber and Stornetta publish secure timestamping for digital documents.
1993  Eric Hughes publishes A Cypherpunk's Manifesto.
1994  Nick Szabo writes about smart contracts and later smart property.
1997  Adam Back announces Hashcash proof-of-work.
1998  Wei Dai proposes b-money; Nick Szabo designs bit gold.
2004  Hal Finney launches RPOW, reusable proofs of work, backed by an IBM 4758.
2008  Satoshi Nakamoto publishes the Bitcoin white paper.
2009  Bitcoin launches; Hal Finney receives the first Bitcoin transaction.
2014  The Declaration of Bitcoin's Independence frames Bitcoin as political exit.
2020s RPOW2 revives the RPOW idea as a modern web tribute; RPOW4 forks and mutates it.
